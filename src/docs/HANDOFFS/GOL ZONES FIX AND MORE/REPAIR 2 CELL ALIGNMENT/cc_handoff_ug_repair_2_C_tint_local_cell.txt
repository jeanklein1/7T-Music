WHY
---
Handoff B fixes where the terrain FS reads its BASE COLOUR. The GoL tint
block, further down the same function, still derives its zone-local cell
from the raw fragment position:

    let rel = in.world_pos.xz - zone_corner;
    let local_cell = vec2<i32>(floor(rel / cell_size));

On a curtain face that position lies exactly on the cell boundary, so
this floors into the neighbour for the same reason B fixes. Landing B
without C leaves the walls with the right base colour and the WRONG
neighbour's life sample: the behaviour tag comes from the owning cell,
the spring value from the cell next door. That reads as a wall tinted
one checker out of step — a partial fix, and more confusing than the
original fault.

The address is already in hand: addr_used. And the arithmetic is exact,
not approximate — cell_size is zp.extent / zp.grid_size, which the U5
derive makes 3.125 identically, and zone_corner is snapped to the same
3.125 grid, so

    local_cell = addr_used - cell_address(zone_corner)

is integer subtraction of two exact quantities. For interior fragments
it is bit-identical to the floor it replaces; for wall fragments it is
correct where the floor was not. Removing the floor removes the only
boundary-sensitive step in the block.

rel and cell_size have no other reader in this block (the bounds check
tests local_cell, and the uv divides by GOL_ZONE_TEX_N since
UG_REPAIR_1 B), so both lets go with it. YAGNI, not hygiene-by-choice.

UG_REPAIR_2 C - the GoL tint derives its cell from the owning address

The tint block computed its zone-local cell by flooring the fragment's
world position, which on a curtain face lies exactly on the cell
boundary. With B landed the wall would have taken its behaviour tag from
the owning cell and its life sample from the neighbour - tinted one

fragments, correct for wall fragments, and free of the only
boundary-sensitive step in the block. rel and cell_size had no other
reader and go with it.
